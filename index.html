The threat

Two ways an anonymous post gives you away

Who wrote this?

Your writing is a fingerprint. In 2013, stylometry helped tie The Cuckoo’s Calling, signed Robert Galbraith, to J. K. Rowling. The small words you cannot stop using do the same to you.

In the demo post, compared with ten writers from the same field:

  • honestly 53× more often
  • basically 40×
  • sorry 27×
  • so 9×
  • anyway 7×

Then there are the details: three years, the Lisbon office, 6 people to 2. Your manager needs no AI for those.

Where was this taken?

A street plaque, a house number, a bakery sign through the window. As posted, a cloud model read all three and placed the demo selfie 215 metres from the spot. After Gemini Nano found the clues and your device pixelated them, it guessed Rome.

Who it is for

  • Employees reviewing their own company.
  • Sources and whistleblowers talking to journalists.
  • Alt accounts where being identified has a cost: health, sexuality, politics.

How it works

A local defender and a cloud attacker

Firebase AI Logic runs Gemini in two places, on purpose. Gemini Nano, on your device, reads your original. Gemini in the cloud only sees what you would post.

your device · private
  1. Fingerprint. Burrows’ Delta over the small words you use, in plain JavaScript. 0 bytes sent.
  2. Find what identifies you. Gemini Nano with ONLY_ON_DEVICE: names, places, dates and counts in a text; plaques, signs and plates in a photo.
  3. Fix it. Fixed rules generalise the details, then Gemini Nano rewrites your style and checks the meaning. Photos are pixelated cell by cell.
release()the only door out
the cloud · what the world will see
  1. Attack. Gemini with ONLY_IN_CLOUD gets the post and ten public writers, shuffled every time, and picks the author. It is never told which one is you. For a photo, it guesses where you are.
  2. Attack again, until it is back to chance: one in ten, or the wrong place.
Private<T>

Your draft or photo. Only local models can read it: a cloud call with it does not compile.

release()

The only way out. It refuses anything that still carries your original, unless you confirm you would post it as it is.

Publishable<T>

The only thing a cloud model accepts. A run-time guard checks every payload anyway.

Privacy

No account. Nothing stored. Nothing to breach.

No login

A tool for staying anonymous should not know who you are. There is no account to create and nothing to sign in to.

Nothing stored, nothing tracked

Your draft and your photo live in this browser tab and are gone when you close it. No analytics, no cookies of ours, and the fonts are bundled.

Only what you choose to test leaves

The first attack measures your post as you would publish it, after you confirm. Finding clues, rewriting and masking all happen on your device.

For developers

A threat is four functions

Throwaway is the reference app of a small TypeScript framework on Firebase AI Logic hybrid inference. You write a threat; the privacy types, the run-time guard and the ledger come with it.

redTeam(threat, input, kit) runs the loop: attack, fix with the attacker’s feedback, attack again, until the attacker stops finding you. Two threats ship today: who wrote a text, and where a photo was taken.

Read the code on GitHub

interface Threat<Input, Clue, Verdict> {
  // on your device
  findClues(input: Private<Input>, kit): Promise<Clue[]>;
  sanitize(input: Private<Input>, clues, feedback, kit):
    Promise<Publishable<Input>>;
  // in the cloud
  attack(published: Publishable<Input>, kit): Promise<Verdict>;
  exposed(verdict: Verdict): boolean;
}

Honest limits

What it cannot promise

  • Stylometry on short texts is noisy. The local score is a signal, not proof.
  • The meaning check runs on a small local model and is lenient. Read the final text before you post it.
  • Names and places are caught only when Gemini Nano flags them. Counts and dates are always generalised.
  • Pixelation hides what a model can read today. Cropping is safer.
  • A provider that sees many attempts could intersect lineups, since you are the one constant. Throwaway limits attempts.
