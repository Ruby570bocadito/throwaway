Who it’s for

Anyone who posts without wanting to be found

Streamers

A window behind you, a street sign, the shop across the road: viewers can piece together where you live. Check a frame before it goes out.

Posts about your work or your street

Your manager, your hospital or your town hall knows how you write. The words you always use, and the details only your people know, can sign the post for you.

Selfies and alt accounts

A house number or a street plaque behind you is an address. Throwaway blurs the clue and leaves your face alone.

How it works

Three steps. The private ones stay on your computer.

  1. 1

    Find

    Paste a post or pick a photo. Gemini Nano, a small AI built into Chrome, looks for what could identify you: the words you always use, names and dates, or a sign behind you.

    On your device
  2. 2

    Fix

    Your post is rewritten in new sentences that keep every point you made, as close to your tone as the small model manages. In a photo, the parts with a clue are blurred; your face stays, unless you ask.

    On your device
  3. 3

    Test

    A bigger AI in the cloud tries to find you, from the version you would post. If it still can, Throwaway fixes more and tests again.

    In the cloud · only what you would post

Privacy, in plain words

What leaves your device, and when

Stays on your device

Your draft and your photo. Finding clues, rewriting and blurring all run inside Chrome, on your computer.

Leaves only when you test

The version you would post goes to Gemini, Google’s AI, on its free plan: Google may use it to improve its products, and people may review it. Your original never leaves your computer.

Kept by us: nothing

No account, no database, no analytics and no cookies of ours. Close the tab and your draft is gone.

Don’t take our word for it

Four ways to check it yourself
  1. Read the code. It is open source, on GitHub.
  2. Count the bytes. In the app, the counter at the top opens a receipt of every step and every byte.
  3. Try to leak it. That receipt has a test button that tries to send your original out. The guard stops it.
  4. Watch the network. In your browser’s developer tools, your post or photo is in no request until you test, and then only the version you would post.

Why Google reCAPTCHA appears: when you open the live app, it checks that requests come from this app and not from a bot, with cookies of Google’s own. It never sees your post or your photo. This site is protected by reCAPTCHA and the Google Privacy Policy and Terms of Service apply, as they do to what you send Gemini in a test.

What you need

On a computer

Chrome 139 or newer, with Gemini Nano. Chrome downloads it the first time, and asks for 22 GB of free disk; the model itself is much smaller.

On a phone or another browser

The demo replays a real check, step by step. You can also check a post with fixed rules, and pick the parts of a photo to blur yourself.

Questions

Before you try it

Is it free? Do I need an account?

It’s free, and there is no account: a tool for staying anonymous shouldn’t know who you are.

Will it blur my face?

Only if you ask. It blurs the clues around you and leaves your face as it is: the point is to post your photo, minus your address. Tick “Blur faces too” and every face it finds is blurred as well.

What exactly leaves my device?

Only the version you would post, and only when you test it. It goes to Gemini in Google’s cloud, which tries to find you. Your original never leaves your computer, and Throwaway itself stores nothing.

What does Google do with what I test?

Tests run on the free plan of Gemini, Google’s AI. Google may use what you test to improve its products, and people at Google may read it. Its terms say not to send anything sensitive there, so only the version you would post goes, never your original.

What is Gemini Nano? Will it download something?

A small AI model from Google, built into Chrome. It runs on your computer, so your draft doesn’t have to go anywhere to be checked or fixed. Chrome downloads it once, when you start your first check. It asks for 22 GB of free disk, though the model itself is much smaller, and removes it by itself if free space drops below 10 GB.

Does it work on my phone?

The demo does, and so do checking a post with fixed rules and picking what to blur in a photo. The full check needs Gemini Nano, which today runs in Chrome on computers.

Can it promise nobody will find me?

No. It lowers the odds against an AI reading your post today. Short posts are hard to judge either way, blurring is weaker than cropping, and the rewrite can still sound more formal than you do. Read the result before you post it.
